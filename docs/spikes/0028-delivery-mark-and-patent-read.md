## 4. The rules-versus-expression line: three cases, summarised

ADR 0007 D7 asks that a statement about litigation cite the case and court and say where it was
read. **These three were read from secondary summaries** (the Wikipedia articles on each case, and
the case-reporter page for *Data East*) **on 2026-10-28, not from the opinions.** They are summarised
because the research of 2026-10-27 §2 used them to say where the line sits. **No legal conclusion is
drawn from them here.**

- ***Tetris Holding, LLC v. Xio Interactive, Inc.***, 863 F. Supp. 2d 394 (D.N.J. 2012). Reported as
  finding that a game whose rules may be copied still infringed where it copied the original's
  expressive look: its pieces, board dimensions and visual details.
- ***Data East USA, Inc. v. Epyx, Inc.***, 862 F.2d 204 (9th Cir. 1988). Reported as finding no
  infringement where the similarities between two karate games came from the sport itself and its
  ideas rather than from protectable expression.
- ***Spry Fox, LLC v. Lolapps, Inc.***, No. 2:12-cv-00147 (W.D. Wash. 2012). Reported as declining to
  dismiss a claim that a re-themed copy of a puzzle game infringed, because a game's overall look and
  feel may be protected even where its rules may not. The case settled. The date of the order was
  not read from the docket.

DL1 is the project's response to that line, chosen by the owner before this write-up: its own name,
art, sounds, words and layouts, and parcels rather than newspapers.

### 5.3 What each claims, in short, from the granted text

- **'417, claims 1, 8, 11, 19, 24, 28, 38, 49, 50 and 51** each claim a **bicycle on a movable support

  simulate uphill and assists pedalling downhill. **Claim 40** is a method: providing a bicycle,
  movably supporting it, sensing its mechanical movement, modelling a simulated bicycle in a *"track
  universe"*, sending a control signal to a feedback motor, and *"providing power assistance from said
  feedback motor to bicyclist pedaling action to simulate downhill movement"*.
- **'219, claims 1, 3, 4, 11, 18, 19 and 21** each require **electro-optically determining data
  concerning points on the person** (or an electro-optical sensor viewing points on the person), while
  the person exercises with an object that resists movement (a barbell, a stretchable cord or a
  squeezable ball in most claims). The data controls a video display.
- **'652 and '752, claims 1 and 15 of each** claim a **system for exercise competition** in which mobile
  devices form a **group of exercisers who share a number of steps taken**, each device tracks its
  exerciser's steps and sends them to another, and each displays the other's.
- **'235, claim 1** claims a **mechanical simulator**: a base, a mounting base, a lifting system, a frame
  with a handlebar, a lower coupling system and a lateral tilting system made of named profiles,
  shafts, tubes and ball joints.

### 5.4 The one disclosure worth recording

The '652 specification, **col. 22, about lines 9 to 31** (read by eye from the granted patent's

character**, conditions in the game used to change the difficulty of exercise, and, for several

exerciser"*. **This is disclosure, not claim scope.** No claim of '652 or '752 reads that way: both
claim step sharing. It is recorded because '752 was filed on 2023-10-17 and granted on 2025-05-06,

## 9. What would make this spike wrong, and when it ages

- **The mark is renewed, assigned or cancelled.** §2.1 is the record as of 2026-10-28. Re-read TSDR
  before relying on it.
- **75881868 turns out to be a live mark for something closer to Delivery.** §2.2 could not say.
