## 1. Is it an "information society service" at all?

The DSA reaches providers of **intermediary services**, and each intermediary service is an
**information society service** (Art. 3(a) and (g)).

- DSA Art. 3(a) points to Directive (EU) 2015/1535, Art. 1(1)(b): *"any service **normally provided
  for remuneration**, at a distance, by electronic means and at the individual request of a recipient
  of services."* DSA recital 5 repeats that definition.
- Directive 2000/31/EC, recital 18: information society services *"in so far as they represent an
  **economic activity**, extend to services which are not remunerated by those who receive them"*,
  and include *"hosting information provided by a recipient of the service"*.

**What the text says.** A service can be an information society service without the recipient
paying, *in so far as* it is an economic activity. **What it leaves open.** Whether a free,
non-commercial service run by one private individual is an economic activity. No text read answers
that, and the case law that might was not read. This is the question most likely to decide scope.
**OPEN, for a lawyer.**

## 2. Does the project "offer services in the Union"?

- Art. 2(1): the Regulation applies to intermediary services offered to recipients *"located in the
  Union, irrespective of where the providers … have their place of establishment"*.
- Art. 3(d)–(e): offering services in the Union means a *"substantial connection to the Union"*,
  which results from establishment or from factual criteria such as *"a significant number of
  recipients … in one or more Member States"* or *"the targeting of activities towards one or more
  Member States"*.
- Recital 8: targeting *"could also be derived from the availability of an application in the relevant
  national application store"*, while *"mere technical accessibility of a website from the Union
  cannot, on that ground alone"* establish a substantial connection.

**Facts the owner holds**: which countries the Android app's store listing covers, and whether EU
riders are expected. **OPEN, for the owner (facts) and a lawyer.** Restricting the project's instance,
or public rooms, to riders outside the EU is an option the owner could take. Its effect on "offering
services in the Union" is not stated in the texts read.

## 3. If it applies: hosting service, or online platform?

- Art. 3(g)(iii): a **hosting service** is *"the storage of information provided by, and at the
  request of, a recipient of the service"*. The instance stores display names, routes shown in rooms
  and results at a rider's request, so on its face it is one.
- Art. 3(i): an **online platform** is a hosting service that *"stores and disseminates information to
  the public"*. Art. 3(k): dissemination to the public is making information available *"to a
  potentially unlimited number of third parties"*.
- **Recital 14**: *"where access to information requires registration or admittance to a group of
  recipients of the service, that information should be considered to be disseminated to the public
  only where recipients of the service seeking to access the information are automatically registered
  or admitted **without a human decision** or selection of whom to grant access."*

**What the text says.** The project's instance uses `approval` registration: a person decides every
account. On recital 14's words, that points **away** from "online platform". **What it leaves open.**
A recital is interpretive, not operative, and whether the public room listing is one of the recital's own examples of something that may still be a
platform, *"public groups or open channels"*, is not settled.
**OPEN, for a lawyer.** A self-hoster running `open` registration may be in a different position.

## 4. If it applies: what Art. 19 does and does not exempt

- **Art. 19(1)**: *"This Section, with the exception of Article 24(3) thereof, shall not apply to
  providers of online platforms that qualify as micro or small enterprises as defined in
  Recommendation 2003/361/EC."* "This Section" is Chapter III Section 3, the additional duties of
  online platforms (Arts 19–28).
- **Art. 15(2)** separately exempts micro and small enterprises from the annual transparency report.
- **Nothing exempts micro or small providers from Arts 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 17 or 18.** That is
  #887's own premise (*"Art. 19 excludes micro and small enterprises from the online platform
  obligations only, not from Art. 16"*), and the text read agrees with it.
- **Rec. 2003/361/EC**, Annex Art. 1: *"An enterprise is considered to be any entity engaged in an
  economic activity, irrespective of its legal form."* Art. 2(3): a microenterprise *"employs fewer
  than 10 persons and whose annual turnover and/or annual balance sheet total does not exceed EUR 2
  million."*

**What the text says.** If the owner is an enterprise, the project is a micro one by the numbers. **What
it leaves open.** The same "economic activity" test as §1 decides whether the owner is an enterprise at
all. If the owner is not an enterprise, the micro and small exemptions, which are written for
enterprises, may be beside the point, because the DSA may not apply in the first place. The text read
does not decide either. **OPEN, for a lawyer.**

**If it applies, and the owner is micro, and the instance is an online platform**: Arts 20–28 do not
apply, except Art. 24(3). That includes the Art. 20 internal complaint-handling system, Art. 23's
suspension for repeat offenders and the Art. 24(5) Transparency Database. Recital 57: nothing prevents a
provider *"setting up, on a voluntary basis, a system that complies with one or more of those
obligations"*.

## 5. If it applies: Arts 11, 12 and 13

- **Arts 11 and 12**: a single point of contact for authorities and one for riders, by electronic
  means, published and kept up to date. Art. 12(1): not *"solely rely on automated tools"*. A page to
  publish them is filed as [#908](https://github.com/openzigs/onyourleft/issues/908).
- **Art. 13(1)**: *"Providers of intermediary services which do not have an establishment in the Union
  but which offer services in the Union shall designate, in writing, a legal or natural person to act
  as their legal representative in one of the Member States where the provider offers its services."*
  Art. 13(3): *"It shall be possible for the designated legal representative to be held liable for
  non-compliance with obligations under this Regulation, without prejudice to the liability and legal
  actions that could be initiated against the provider of intermediary services."* Art. 13(4): their name,
  postal address, email address and telephone number go to the Digital Services Coordinator of that
  Member State and are published.
- The owner is in the UK. **If** the DSA applies, Art. 13 requires a real person or company in a Member
  State, who can be held liable. That is a cost and a person, and **no micro or small exemption applies
  to it**. **OPEN, for the owner.**

## 6. The owner's decision

**On 2026-09-30 the owner decided to treat the DSA as probably not applying** (a free service, one
person, not provided for remuneration). **Articles 16 and 17 are followed voluntarily anyway**,
through the [notice-and-action procedure](notice-and-action.md) and the
[statements of reasons](statement-of-reasons.md).

This was option 3 of the draft, which recorded what it means: those two documents are needed for the
UK Act in any case, so most of the cost is paid already; no Art. 13 representative is appointed; and
the risk is being wrong about §1 or §2. The open points below are the questions that could change
this position.
