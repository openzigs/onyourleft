# Trademarks

"On Your Left", the On Your Left wordmark, the shield-and-rider logo and the app icon are the marks
of the On Your Left project. This page says how you may and may not use them. It is short on purpose,
and it follows the shape of [Mozilla's trademark policy](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/foundation/trademarks/policy/):
the code is free; the name is how people know what they are getting.

## The code and the art are licensed. The name is not.

- The code is `AGPL-3.0-or-later` under `apps/` and `Apache-2.0` under `packages/` —
  [README §Licence](README.md#licence).
- The logo, the wordmark and the icon are the owner's art, made with Google Gemini and published
  under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). [`ASSETS.toml`](ASSETS.toml)
  records each file and how it was made.
- **None of those licences grants any right in the marks.** CC BY 4.0 says so itself, in
  §2(b)(2): *"Patent and trademark rights are not licensed under this Public License."*
  Apache-2.0 §6 says the same of trademarks. So copying the art under CC BY does not let you use it
  **as a trademark** — to name or brand a product. The two do not conflict; they cover different
  things.

## What you may do without asking

- **Say what is true.** "Based on On Your Left", "a fork of On Your Left", "compatible with On Your
  Left", "works with an On Your Left instance", or a link to this repository. Plain, factual
  references are always fine, in text, in a store listing or in a talk.
- **Run, study, change and share the software**, as its licences allow — including an unmodified
  build, under its own name, and an instance you host for yourself and your friends.
- **Show the logo when you write about the project**, unaltered, as long as it is clear you are
  talking about this project and not speaking for it.

## What you may not do

- **Pass something off as On Your Left.** A fork, a modified build or a hosted service you run may
  not use the name, the wordmark, the logo or the icon as its own name or brand, or in a way that
  suggests it is the original or is endorsed by this project. Rename it and give it its own icon.
  Saying "based on On Your Left" in its description is fine (above).
- **Use a confusingly similar name or mark** — a variation, an abbreviation or a lookalike icon —
  for a cycling app or service.
- **Use the marks in a domain name, an app-store developer name or a social account** that could be
  taken for the project's own.

## Why

The licences guarantee anybody can run and change this software, and they should. The marks exist
so that a rider who installs "On Your Left" gets this project — its privacy promises, its safety
rules for a trainer that pushes back on your legs — and not a build that made different choices
under the same name. The AGPL already ensures you can see the source of any hosted fork; this page
ensures you can tell which one it is.

If you are not sure whether a use is fine, open an issue and ask.
